Case Study 01 · Information design

Visual Codes
for Milk

A visual language that replaces fine print with shapes, color, and texture, so any shopper can compare milk from across the aisle without reading a word.

Context
Graphic Systems, University of Georgia
Discipline
Information design, packaging, iconography
Deliverables
Visual code system, 7 carton codes, system key
Year
2026

Challenge

Milk packaging asks people to read small print to compare products. In a busy aisle shoppers decide in seconds, and anyone with low literacy or low vision is left out entirely.

Approach

I broke nutrition down into four layers of information and gave each one a familiar visual metaphor: color for variety, density for richness, a ring for protein, and fullness for calories. Together they form a single carton-shaped code.

Outcome

A barcode alternative that tells seven milk varieties apart using only shapes, colors, and textures. Its modular logic is built to extend to other grocery categories.

The system

Four layers. One glance.

  • Color Milk type

    Blue for dairy; warm, earthy tones for plant-based options.

  • Dot density Fat content

    Sparse dots for low-fat, dense dots for whole milk.

  • Ring Protein

    An empty ring means 0 g of protein; a full ring means 8 g.

  • Fill level Calories

    The fuller the carton looks, the more calories it has (0–150).

Case Study 02 · Exhibition campaign

Eye on
Conservation

A three-poster campaign for the Georgia Museum of Natural History that invites people into the collection and shows off Georgia’s remarkable diversity of plants and animals.

Client
Georgia Museum of Natural History
Discipline
Campaign design, illustration, typography
Deliverables
Three-poster series, campaign system
Year
2026

Challenge

Natural history collections are easy to walk past. The campaign had to make conservation feel alive and urgent, not archival, and give students and the public a reason to visit.

Approach

One bold two-color palette, one illustrated fish, three compositions. The fish moves through each poster in a new position, while a consistent type lockup carries the hours, address, and contact details.

Outcome

A flexible poster system that reads as one campaign when hung in a row and still holds its own as a single poster on a crowded campus board.

Campaign toolkit

Built to repeat.

  • Field Green #2EB542
  • Specimen Pink #FFD8D3
  • One hero illustration Rotated, scaled and cropped to give each poster its own composition
  • Fixed information lockup Title, hours, address, and contact always set the same way

Case Study 03 · Event branding

Pike’s
Peak

Promotional posters for Pi Kappa Alpha’s Pike’s Peak concert, designed to set the mood for the night and stand out in a crowded social feed.

Client
Pi Kappa Alpha, University of Georgia
Discipline
Event branding, poster design, social
Deliverables
Lineup poster (1080×1920 story), headliner poster
Year
2026

Challenge

A one-night event competes with every other party on campus. The poster has a couple of seconds on a phone screen to sell the mood, the date, and the lineup.

Approach

Each poster plays on the “peak” in its own way: vintage concert-bill type over a painted mountain for the lineup, and a snowy, illustrated chapter house for the headliner. Both set the schedule as clean, time-stamped information that’s easy to scan.

Outcome

Two posters that give the event a recognizable mountain personality: a full-lineup version built natively for Instagram Stories and a headliner announcement.
