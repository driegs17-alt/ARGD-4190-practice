Stuff They Don’t Want You to Know

Brand identity and episode cover system for a podcast on conspiracies, cover-ups and unexplained history.

Context
Concept rebrand, University of Georgia
Role
Identity design, art direction
Deliverables
Logotype, show cover, episode system, guidelines
Tools
Photoshop, InDesign

Overview

The podcast covers what is missing from the official record. The identity is built on the redaction bar, the most recognizable sign of withheld information, and applies it to the show’s own name.

The brief called for a logotype and show cover, extended into a visual system and guidelines. The challenge was to avoid the genre’s usual cues (grain, glitch effects, alarm red) and create something that reads as credible and documentary, while staying legible as a small thumbnail in a podcast feed.

Process

01 Initial concept

The first cover was typographic: a bracket frame with a black bar replacing the word “Want.” The concept communicated immediately, but the heavy capitals and saturated red read closer to a tabloid than an investigation.

02 Symbol exploration

I explored a secondary mark: a Pacific Northwest pine constructed from redaction bars, with a single orange bar left uncensored. It was extended into illustrated episode covers. The mark was retained; the illustrated covers were not, because producing a new drawing each week was not sustainable.

03 Refinement

The final cover keeps the original composition and adopts the exploration’s palette and typography. Heavy capitals were replaced with Instrument Serif, the red was shifted to a warmer orange, and black to a deep green-black. The result reads as editorial rather than sensational.

04 Episode system

Each episode cover is produced from one template. The category sets the background color, the title is set in two or three lines, and one keyword is placed in the redaction bar. The brackets and file line stay fixed so every episode is recognizable at small sizes.

Applications

Outcome

The final identity scales from a 3000 px show cover to a watch display using one consistent idea. The key decision was choosing a repeatable template over illustrated covers, so the system can be maintained week to week without a designer redrawing each episode.

Concept work created for a university design course. Not affiliated with the show or its publisher.
