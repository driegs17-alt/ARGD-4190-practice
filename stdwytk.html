Stuff They Don’t
Want You to Know

Brand identity and episode cover system for a podcast on conspiracies and unexplained history.

Role
Identity design
Context
University of Georgia
Tools
Photoshop, InDesign
Year
2026

The brief

Design a logotype and show cover for a podcast about what is missing from the official record, then extend it into a system. The goal was to avoid the genre’s usual grain, glitch and alarm red, and look credible instead.

From first draft to final cover

The core idea came first: a redaction bar in place of the word “Want.” The first draft used heavy capitals and bright red, which felt like a tabloid. The final cover keeps the composition and switches to an editorial serif and a calmer palette.

Exploring a symbol

To give the show a sense of place, I built a secondary mark: a Pacific Northwest pine made from redaction bars, with one bar left uncensored in orange. I also tested illustrated episode covers in the same style. The mark stayed in the system, but a new illustration every week was not sustainable.

One template for every episode

The final system replaces illustration with a template. Every cover uses the same bracket frame and serif title. Five story categories each get their own background color, and one keyword per title is placed in the redaction bar, so a new cover takes minutes.

In use

The final presentation shows the identity across the show cover, a listening app and a watch.

Outcome

One idea carries the identity from a 3000 px show cover down to a watch face. The biggest decision was choosing a repeatable template over illustrated covers, so the show can publish weekly without redrawing anything.

Concept work for a university design course. Not affiliated with the show or its publisher.
