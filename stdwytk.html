Stuff They Don’t
Want You to Know

Project
Podcast rebrand
Role
Identity design, art direction
Deliverables
Show cover, episode system, guidelines
Context
University of Georgia

01 — Overview

A podcast about what is missing from the official record. So the identity redacts its own name.

The challenge

Conspiracy media leans on the same clichés: grain, glitch effects and alarm red. The show needed to look credible and documentary, and still read clearly as a small thumbnail in a podcast feed.

Objectives

  1. Build the identity on one simple, ownable idea.
  2. Feel investigative and editorial, not sensational.
  3. Make weekly episode covers fast to produce.

Read between the lines.

02 — Process

From a tabloid draft to an editorial system.

Sketching

Every direction started on paper. Early notebook sketches tested how a redaction could live inside the name itself, before anything went near the computer.

Notebook sketch 1
Notebook sketch 2
Notebook sketch 3

First draft

The core idea arrived early: a black bar in place of “Want.” Set in heavy capitals and bright red, it worked, but it read like a tabloid headline.

Exploration

To give the show a sense of place, I built a Pacific Northwest pine from redaction bars, with one bar left uncensored. Illustrated episode covers tested the idea further, but drawing a new one every week wasn’t sustainable.

Refinement

The final cover keeps the first draft’s composition and borrows the exploration’s palette and type. An editorial serif replaces the capitals, and “Don’t” becomes an italic whisper.

04 — Episode System

One frame. One redacted word.

Every cover is built from the same template. The story category sets the color, and the word a listener would search for goes in the bar. A new episode takes minutes, not a new illustration.

06 — Outcome

One idea carries the brand from a 3000 px cover to a watch face, and a weekly show can run it without redrawing anything.
